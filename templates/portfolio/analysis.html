What sits behind these numbers

Resilience, risk and historical scenarios

Score v1 = 100 × [0.4 × max(0, 1 − annualized volatility / 0.60) + 0.4 × max(0, 1 − |maximum drawdown| / 0.60) + 0.2 × (1 − sum of squared portfolio weights))]. Cash is included in concentration. Returns use 252 periods per year and a 0% risk-free rate. This score is heuristic and has not been calibrated to a probability of loss.

Stress tests freeze today's asset weights and replay the endpoints of a historical window. They are hypothetical exposure analyses, not the actual historical performance of your account. Demo paths are synthetic and do not reproduce actual crash returns.

Model training, evaluation and limitations

The default random forest uses lagged returns, 5/20-period momentum, a 20-period moving-average gap and volatility to predict the next observation's return. The final 20% of data is held out, with a one-observation purge. Three expanding-window splits evaluate training stability. A zero-return forecast is the baseline. A separate estimator is then refit on available labeled observations.

The model strategy holds the asset when the forecast exceeds 0.1%, otherwise cash, charging 0.1% per position change. Backtests omit slippage, taxes and settlement constraints. Same-close execution is an optimistic simplifying assumption. Forecasts have no calibrated confidence interval. The estimator can be replaced through ML_MODEL_CLASS.
